# City General Hospital — Emergency Services Guide

## When to Visit the Emergency Department
The Emergency Department (ED) should be used for conditions that are life-threatening or require immediate medical attention, including:
- Chest pain or difficulty breathing
- Severe bleeding that cannot be controlled
- Signs of stroke (sudden numbness, confusion, trouble speaking, severe headache)
- Serious injuries from accidents, falls, or burns
- Loss of consciousness or fainting
- Severe allergic reactions (anaphylaxis)
- Poisoning or drug overdose
- High fever (above 104°F) that does not respond to medication
- Seizures

## When to Use Urgent Care or Same-Day Clinic Instead
For conditions that need prompt attention but are not life-threatening, consider our same-day urgent clinic:
- Minor cuts requiring stitches
- Sprains and minor fractures
- Moderate fever or flu symptoms
- Urinary tract infections
- Ear infections
- Mild allergic reactions (rash, hives without breathing difficulty)

## What to Expect at the Emergency Department
1. **Triage:** Upon arrival, a triage nurse will assess your condition and assign a priority level. Patients are seen based on medical severity, not arrival order.
2. **Registration:** You will be registered and asked for your ID and insurance information. Emergency care is never denied due to inability to pay.
3. **Evaluation:** A physician will examine you and may order tests such as blood work, imaging, or ECG.
4. **Treatment:** You will receive appropriate treatment. This may include medication, procedures, or admission to the hospital.
5. **Discharge or Admission:** Once stabilized, you will either be discharged with follow-up instructions or admitted for further care.

## Emergency Department Hours
Open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, 365 days a year.
